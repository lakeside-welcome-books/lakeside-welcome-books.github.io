🚲Trail of the Coeur d’Alenes

The Trail of the Coeur d’Alenes is a free, paved, 73-mile rail-trail between Mullan and Plummer. In Harrison it runs right through town between the city park and the public beach and marina. The Harrison trailhead is at mile 15.3. Class 1 and 2 e-bikes are allowed.

Getting on the trail

  • Trail parking
    On Lakefront Ave., across from One Shot Charlie’s. The big lot below is for campground guests, boaters, and Gateway Marina customers. Drivers with a disability placard can park in the ADA spot by the trail entrance near the beach.
  • Restrooms
    The vault toilet at the trailhead (across from the public beach) is open year-round and ADA accessible. Restrooms in the northeast corner of City Park are open seasonally.
  • Quieter parking
    Springston Trailhead, about a 4-mile drive: north on Hwy 97, cross the bridge, right on Blue Lake Rd for 1.8 miles, then right over Springston Bridge. It has 13 spaces and an accessible restroom.

Bike rentals

  • The Cycle Haus: Bikes & Brews
    100 N Coeur d’Alene Ave · e-bikes, regular and kids’ bikes, trailers, repairs, espresso. Open early spring through late fall. (208) 689-3436

Rides from here

  • South to Chatcolet Bridge & Heyburn State Park
    About 7 to 7.5 miles each way along the lake and over the long Chatcolet Bridge into Heyburn, the oldest state park in the Pacific Northwest (created in 1908).
  • East up the Coeur d’Alene River
    Quiet wetlands and chain lakes. Watch for moose east of town. There’s no drinking water for the roughly 30 miles between Harrison and Cataldo, so carry plenty.
Play clean. The old rail bed was built with mine waste. The trail’s asphalt and gravel edges act as a clean cap, and the area is part of the Coeur d’Alene Basin cleanup. Stay on the trail and at designated rest areas. Wash hands and faces before eating, bring your own drinking water (never drink lake or river water, even filtered), and brush dirt off shoes, gear, and pets before coming inside. Advice from Idaho Parks & Recreation and the Coeur d’Alene Basin cleanup program.

⛵Marina, boat launch & beach

  • Harrison Docks & public boat launch
    250 W Harrison St · run by Kootenai County Parks & Waterways. Launch fee or county launch pass required. Free moorage with a 48-hour limit; quiet hours 10 PM to 8 AM; pump-out available. (208) 446-1275
  • The Gateway Marina
    250 W Harrison St · boat slips, auto and watercraft fuel, invasive-species boat inspections and stickers, and the Lakeside Bar & Grill. (208) 682-8036
  • Harrison Idaho Water Adventures
    Rentals of boats, kayaks, water toys, and paddleboards. (208) 582-0177
  • Public beach
    Sandy beach, wading and swimming area, and picnic tables down by the marina.
Bringing a boat, kayak, or canoe? Idaho requires an Invasive Species sticker on all of them, including kayaks, canoes, and drift boats. The docks fill up fast on hot summer days.

🍂Seasonal tips

Summer

Harrison’s busy season. The public docks fill up fast on hot days, so launch early. The Old Time Picnic, a community tradition, is held the last weekend of July.

Spring & fall

Bike rentals typically run early spring through late fall, and the Crane House Museum is mostly a summer-weekend stop. Birders like the Thompson Lake Wildlife Refuge at the edge of town for osprey, eagles, and swans.

Winter

A quiet, peaceful lake town. Some places cut back their hours: the Chamber lists One Shot Charlie’s as open Friday to Sunday in winter and the Trading Post as open 9 AM to 6 PM. Call ahead.

Fishing

Idaho Health & Welfare and the Coeur d’Alene Tribe advise eating limited amounts of fish from Coeur d’Alene Lake and the Coeur d’Alene Basin, especially for children and pregnant or nursing women.
